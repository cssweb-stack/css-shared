# Language & Writing

These rules guide writing and reviewing university websites. Improve clarity and consistency while preserving academic meaning. This is the editable rule sheet displayed on Review.

## Voice & Introductions

- Use third person for ordinary department and program descriptions. Do not switch casually between students, applicants and you in the same passage.
- Preserve the intended voice of faculty biographies, attributed quotations and unit-authored identity statements. Anthropology’s homepage statement and Ethnic Studies’ Our History, Our Way are protected examples. Ask before rewriting protected text or text whose authorship or purpose is unclear.
- Use they, them and their for a person whose gender is unspecified, with matching verbs. Replace generic he or she, him or her and his or her. Keep accurate pronouns about named people.
- Keep introductions short, broad and grounded in the page. When an intro is missing, write one without simply listing the sections below or adding unsupported claims. Avoid unnecessary repetition and details that create ongoing maintenance.

## Institutional & Program Names

- Identify the unit clearly and use its full name and established acronym where useful. Use University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa for formal institutional identification and UH Mānoa for shorter or subsequent references. Do not use UHM as ordinary shorthand.
- Name placement is strategic, not a quota. Do not force the full university name, unit name and College of Social Sciences into every page intro. Formal pages such as homepages and support pages are useful places for fuller identification. Mention the college when establishing affiliation or where it fits naturally, without repetitive name-dropping.
- Preserve the approved full-name homepage welcome where used. Protected unit-authored statements, including Anthropology’s homepage, remain exceptions to imposing standard welcome copy.
- Degree pages should clearly identify both the subject and the credential. Full degree names, familiar abbreviations such as BA or MA, and terms such as major should appear naturally where relevant. Do not assume which wording students search for without evidence or force every variant into every heading.
- Concise navigation and more descriptive H1s may differ where approved, such as Major (BA) and Bachelor of Arts in Anthropology. There is no newly imposed universal degree-H1 formula. SEO review checks titles, headings and terminology together; filename changes require separate approval and redirect planning.
- Capitalize full official names. Lowercase generic references in prose, such as the department, graduate chair, graduate specialist, anthropology courses and a minor in anthropology.
- Define unfamiliar acronyms when they will be reused. Do not add unused acronyms. When the H1 already defines one, the intro may start with the acronym instead of repeating the full name.
- Why Study navigation uses the unit acronym and a question mark; its H1 spells out the subject and keeps the question mark. Example: Why Study ANTH? → Why Study Anthropology?

## Hawaiian Spelling

- Check known Hawaiian spelling and diacritics in headings, body copy, page titles, alt text and link labels. Examples include Hawaiʻi, Mānoa and Oʻahu.
- Use the correct ʻokina and kahakō where the spelling is known. Do not substitute a backtick for an ʻokina or guess unfamiliar personal or place names. Flag uncertain spellings for confirmation.
- Preserve protected wording and official names as appropriate. Do not add diacritics to URLs, filenames, IDs or code identifiers as part of a language pass.

## Courses & Punctuation

- Write named courses as ANTH 399: Directed Reading or Research, with a space between subject and number and a colon plus space before the title.
- Preserve course titles, suffixes, cross-listings, topic markers, credits and restrictions. Code-only references such as ANTH 399 do not need an invented title or colon. Check conflicting titles against an approved source rather than guessing.
- Use a spaced em dash for directory entries: Name — Research interests. Do not substitute a hyphen or colon.
- Run-in explanatory labels use sentence case, bold and a colon: **Save money:** Explanation. Do not alternate between label-period, label-dash and italic-label treatments for equivalent items.
- Do not introduce prose dashes unnecessarily. Preserve meaningful hyphens, numeric ranges and protected wording. E komo mai — Welcome to… is an approved exception.
- Use & in concise navigation labels and headings where approved; use and in ordinary prose. Preserve official organization names. For the same page topic, keep the approved & or and choice consistent between navigation and H1, including the topic after any program prefix. Do not silently expand & to and in the H1. Keep local page-title wording aligned unless an approved search-title exception applies.
- Keep punctuation beside the preceding word or link. Code Formatting’s Whitespace subsection governs accidental double spaces, nonbreaking spaces, spaces around inline tags and spaces before punctuation.
- Preserve meaningful footnote markers. Use a space after a leading asterisk: * This applies…

## Headings & Emphasis

- Use title case for short headings. Use sentence case for full-sentence questions and explanatory labels. Do not add trailing colons or periods to short heading labels; retain question marks on questions.
- Use real headings rather than bold paragraphs pretending to be headings. Shared CSS controls heading size and weight. Do not put bold or italic tags inside headings.
- Avoid repeating the H1 as the next content heading. Use a useful subsection label or begin the content directly.
- Use bullets or numbered lists when prose contains separate requirements or steps. Preserve qualifications, exceptions, totals, dates and obligations.
- Keep italics limited and purposeful. Preserve journal titles and protected emphasis; remove unnecessary italics during an authorized edit. Use bold sparingly for useful labels, not whole paragraphs.

## Links & Review

- Link meaningful words rather than raw URLs or redundant Visit… sentences.
- Profile website sections use Online. Use Website for a personal site when approved; preserve useful labels such as Website/CV and Lab Website. Keep email and phone icons; website links do not use a globe.
- Generic graduate chair and graduate specialist references link to the unit’s Contact page. Preserve approved program inboxes and named coordinator links. Do not change departmental inquiry routing without approval.
- A requested language cleanup authorizes obvious grammar, punctuation, voice, pronoun and repetition corrections. Ask when a change may affect facts, policy, requirements or implications, or when the meaning is uncertain. A request to read and propose edits does not authorize applying them.
- Report what was actually checked and any unresolved questions. Do not claim a whole-site language, accessibility or SEO pass after reviewing a few pages.
- This sheet owns language decisions. Code Formatting owns source formatting and mechanical whitespace. SEO review checks how approved terminology appears in titles and headings; Website Construction governs paths. Those procedures must not invent competing naming rules.
- If another document conflicts with this sheet, flag the conflict rather than silently combining instructions. Resolve unclear decisions with the user. After resolution, remove or update the obsolete instruction at its source. Preserve historical records as history, clearly marked as superseded.
